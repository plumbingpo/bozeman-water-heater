No Surprises

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

No homeowner likes a surprise on the invoice. That's why Bozeman Water Heater prices every job upfront, after a full diagnostic that covers the heater's age, fuel type, and capacity, the elements or burner, the anode rod and relief valve, and the venting and supply lines. You'll know what's wrong, what it takes to fix, and exactly what it will cost before you say yes.

Most water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Keep an eye out for discolored or rusty hot water, a rumbling tank, hot water that smells like rotten eggs, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a spent anode rod, or a worn dip tube. An early service call at the first sign often costs far less than mopping up after a ruptured tank.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The serial number on the label usually tells us. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth scheduling a replacement before the current one forces the issue. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Each maintenance visit from Bozeman Water Heater is far more than opening the drain valve. The technician flushes the sediment, pulls and inspects the anode rod, tests the relief valve, inspects the burner, flue, and draft on gas models, checks elements and thermostats on electric models, and sets a safe water temperature. Small problems are caught while they're still cheap to fix, and your water heater stays running at full efficiency.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment coats the tank floor, forces the burner to run longer, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your equipment's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

Consider maintenance as protection for equipment you've already bought. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod doing its job, and the relief valve ready to open, and that lowers monthly energy bills while reducing wear on the burner or elements. For most families, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can hold when the incoming water is cold. We don't simply copy the gallon rating of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely holds out after two teenagers start showering back to back, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water so long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: installed price, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical service, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Bozeman

Most service calls we get come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The gas burner won't light, or it lights and shuts off. The shower goes cold after a few minutes, the tank rumbles and pops, or the tankless unit locks out with an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. Our technicians handle all of them, always targeting the component that actually failed.

Some failures can wait a day, and some can't. A failed element just cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve spitting water, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and a slow leak at the base can soak into subfloors and drywall before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. We come prepared to swap elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and make leak repairs at fittings and drain valves.
