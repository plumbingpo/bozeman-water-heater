How Livingston Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a flooded utility room.
